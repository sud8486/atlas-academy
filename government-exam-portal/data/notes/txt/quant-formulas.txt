  1. Calendar
  Every calendar question reduces to counting odd days (days left over after complete
  weeks), then moving forward from a known day.


     Concept                                       Formula / Value

     Odd days                                      Total days ÷ 7 → remainder

     Ordinary year (365 days)                      1 odd day

     Leap year (366 days)                          2 odd days

     100 years                                     5 odd days

     200 years                                     3 odd days

     300 years                                     1 odd day

     400 years                                     0 odd days

     Leap years in 100 years                       24 (100th year not leap unless divisible by 400)


  Leap year test: non-century year divisible by 4; century year divisible by 400 (2000 leap,
  1900 not leap).

  Odd-day code (0 to 6): 0 = Sunday, 1 = Monday, 2 = Tuesday, 3 = Wednesday, 4 = Thursday,
  5 = Friday, 6 = Saturday.

  Month odd days (ordinary year): Jan 3, Feb 0, Mar 3, Apr 2, May 3, Jun 2, Jul 3, Aug 3, Sep
  2, Oct 3, Nov 2, Dec 3 (Feb = 1 in a leap year).

  Month codes for quick day finding (ordinary year): Jan 0, Feb 3, Mar 3, Apr 6, May 1, Jun
  4, Jul 6, Aug 2, Sep 5, Oct 0, Nov 3, Dec 5. In a leap year, Jan = 6 and Feb = 2.

  Quick shortcuts

         Same date next year moves forward 1 day (2 days if Feb 29 falls in between).

         Calendar of a year repeats: ordinary year after leap year → +6 years; year after that
         (2nd after leap) → +11 years; 3rd after leap → +11 years; leap year → +28 years.
         First and last day of an ordinary year are the same day; in a leap year the last day is one
         day ahead of the first.
         A century's last day can only be Monday, Wednesday, Friday or Sunday.


  2. Clock
  The minute hand gains 5.5° per minute over the hour hand; almost every clock question
  uses this.


     Concept                                           Formula / Value

     Minute hand speed                                 6° per minute (360° in 60 min)

     Hour hand speed                                   0.5° per minute (30° per hour)

     Relative speed                                    5.5° per minute, i.e. 55 minute-spaces gained
                                                       in 60 min

     Angle between hands at H hours M minutes          θ = |30H − 5.5M| (if > 180°, take 360° − θ)

     Time when hands make angle θ between H            M = (30H ± θ) × 2/11 minutes past H
     and H+1

     Hands coincide between H and H+1                  M = 60H/11 minutes past H

     Hands opposite (180°)                             M = (30H ± 180) × 2/11

     Hands at right angle (90°)                        M = (30H ± 90) × 2/11

     Hands coincide every                              65 5/11 minutes


  Counts in a day (24 hours)

         Coincide: 22 times (11 in 12 hours)
         Opposite (straight line, 180°): 22 times
         Right angle: 44 times (22 in 12 hours)
         Straight line (coincide + opposite): 44 times

  Fast / slow clock: if hands meet every T minutes instead of 65 5/11, the clock gains or loses
  (720/11 − T) × (60 × 24 / T) minutes per day — gains if T < 65 5/11, loses if T > 65 5/11.

  3. Profit and Loss
  Profit and loss percentages are always calculated on Cost Price (CP) unless the question
  says otherwise. SP = Selling Price.


     Concept                                            Formula

     Profit                                             SP − CP

     Loss                                               CP − SP

     Profit %                                           (Profit ÷ CP) × 100

     Loss %                                             (Loss ÷ CP) × 100

     SP when profit P%                                  CP × (100 + P) ÷ 100

     SP when loss L%                                    CP × (100 − L) ÷ 100

     CP from SP (profit P%)                             SP × 100 ÷ (100 + P)

     CP from SP (loss L%)                               SP × 100 ÷ (100 − L)

     Two items sold at same SP, one at x% gain          Always a loss of x²/100 %
     and other at x% loss

     Successive profits a% and b%                       Net % = a + b + ab/100 (use −b for a loss)

     False weight (dishonest dealer selling at CP)      Gain % = (True wt − False wt) ÷ False wt ×
                                                        100

     False weight + profit/loss on price                (100 + gain%) ÷ (100 − loss%) = True wt ÷
                                                        False wt

     CP of x items = SP of y items                      Profit % = (x − y) ÷ y × 100 (loss if y > x)

     Buy x get y free                                   Discount % = y ÷ (x + y) × 100


  Shortcuts

         If selling at ₹A gives the same % profit as selling at ₹B gives % loss: CP = (A + B) ÷ 2.
         If profit at SP₁ is P₁ and at SP₂ is P₂ (same item): CP = (SP₁ − SP₂) × 100 ÷ (P₁ − P₂).

  4. Simple Interest
  Simple interest (SI) is charged only on the original principal, so it is the same every year. P
  = Principal, R = rate % per annum, T = time in years, A = Amount.


     Concept                                             Formula

     Simple Interest                                     SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100

     Amount                                              A = P + SI = P × (100 + RT) ÷ 100

     Principal                                           P = (SI × 100) ÷ (R × T)

     Rate                                                R = (SI × 100) ÷ (P × T)

     Time                                                T = (SI × 100) ÷ (P × R)

     Sum becomes n times in T years                      R = (n − 1) × 100 ÷ T

     Sum becomes n times at R%                           T = (n − 1) × 100 ÷ R

     Sum becomes n₁ times in T₁ years; to become         T₂ = (n₂ − 1) ÷ (n₁ − 1) × T₁
     n₂ times

     Amount A₁ after T₁ years and A₂ after T₂            SI per year = (A₂ − A₁) ÷ (T₂ − T₁); P = A₁ − T₁ ×
     years                                               (SI per year)

     Equal annual instalment x to repay debt A in        A = x·T + (x·R ÷ 100) × [T(T − 1) ÷ 2]
     T years


  Shortcuts

         Time in months → divide by 12; days → divide by 365.
         Sum doubles (n = 2): R × T = 100. Triples: R × T = 200.
         SI for each year is equal, so SI for 1 year = Total SI ÷ T.


  5. Compound Interest
  Compound interest (CI) adds each year's interest to the principal, so interest grows every
  year: A = P(1 + R/100)ᵀ and CI = A − P.

     Concept                                                         Formula

     Amount (annual)                                                 A = P × (1 + R/100)ᵀ

     Compound Interest                                               CI = P × [(1 + R/100)ᵀ − 1]

     Half-yearly compounding                                         A = P × (1 + R/200)²ᵀ

     Quarterly compounding                                           A = P × (1 + R/400)⁴ᵀ

     Different rates each year R₁, R₂, R₃                            A = P × (1 + R₁/100)(1 + R₂/100)(1 +
                                                                     R₃/100)

     Fractional time, e.g. 2⅓ years                                  A = P × (1 + R/100)² × (1 + (R/3)/100)

     CI − SI for 2 years                                             D = P × (R/100)²

     CI − SI for 3 years                                             D = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)

     CI for 2 years at R% (net %)                                    2R + R²/100

     Sum becomes n times in T years; becomes nᵏ                      k × T years
     times in

     Amount A₁ after T years, A₂ after (T + 1) years                 R = (A₂ − A₁) ÷ A₁ × 100

     Depreciation (value falls R% yearly)                            Value after T years = P × (1 − R/100)ᵀ

     Population growth                                               Population after T years = P × (1 +
                                                                     R/100)ᵀ

     Equal annual instalment x (2 years)                             P = x ÷ (1 + R/100) + x ÷ (1 + R/100)²


  Ready values (2 years)


     Rate        CI % for 2 years               CI − SI (per ₹100)

     5%          10.25%                         ₹0.25

     10%         21%                            ₹1

     15%         32.25%                         ₹2.25

     20%         44%                            ₹4


  Shortcut: at 10% for 3 years, CI = 33.1% of P; at 20% for 3 years, CI = 72.8% of P.

  6. Average
  Average = Sum of observations ÷ Number of observations; most questions turn on how
  the sum changes.


     Concept                                              Formula

     Average                                              Sum ÷ n

     Sum                                                  Average × n

     First n natural numbers                              (n + 1) ÷ 2

     First n even numbers                                 n+1

     First n odd numbers                                  n

     Squares of first n natural numbers                   (n + 1)(2n + 1) ÷ 6

     Cubes of first n natural numbers                     n(n + 1)² ÷ 4

     Consecutive numbers / any arithmetic series          (First term + Last term) ÷ 2

     Combined average of two groups                       (n₁A₁ + n₂A₂) ÷ (n₁ + n₂)

     Average speed, equal distances at x and y            2xy ÷ (x + y)

     Average speed, equal distances at x, y, z            3xyz ÷ (xy + yz + zx)

     New person replaces one; average changes by d        New value = Old value ± n × d

     New person joins (n becomes n + 1); average          New value = Old average ± (n + 1) × d
     changes by d

     Person leaves (n becomes n − 1); average             Value of leaver = Old average ∓ (n − 1) ×
     changes by d                                         d


  Shortcuts

         Adding (or multiplying) every value by k adds (or multiplies) the average by k.
         Cricket: new average after an innings of x runs rising by d → old average = x − (n + 1)d,
         where n = innings before.
         Overlapping groups (e.g. first 6 and last 6 of 11 results): shared middle value = (sum of
         first part + sum of last part) − total sum.

  7. Age
  The difference between two people's ages never changes; ratio questions are solved by
  keeping that difference fixed.


     Concept                                           Formula

     Present age x                                     n years ago = x − n; after n years = x + n

     Age is n times another person's                   Elder = n × Younger

     Present ratio a : b, after n years ratio c : d    Ages = a·k and b·k, where k = n(c − d) ÷ (ad − bc)

     Present ratio a : b, n years ago ratio c : d      k = n(d − c) ÷ (ad − bc), ages = a·k and b·k

     Sum of ages S, ratio a : b                        Ages = S·a ÷ (a + b) and S·b ÷ (a + b)

     Average age of n people, after t years            New average = Old average + t

     Sum of ages of n people after t years             Old sum + n·t

     Person born when other was y years old            Difference in ages = y (always)


  Shortcuts

         Ratio method: make the ratio difference equal in both ratios (e.g. 3 : 5 and 5 : 7 →
         difference 2 in both), then 1 ratio part = years passed ÷ change in ratio term.
         Example: ratio 3 : 5 now, 5 : 7 after 6 years → each part moves by 2 for 6 years → 1 part =
         3 years → ages 9 and 15.
         Back-solve from options: in UPSSSC MCQs, testing each option against the ratio is
         often fastest.


  8. Pipe and Cistern
  A pipe that fills a tank in x hours does 1/x of the tank per hour; inlet work is positive,
  outlet (leak) work is negative.


     Concept                                              Formula

     Inlet fills in x hours                               Work per hour = +1/x

     Outlet empties in y hours                            Work per hour = −1/y

     Concept                                           Formula

     Two inlets A (x hrs) and B (y hrs) together       Time = xy ÷ (x + y)

     Inlet x hrs, outlet y hrs (y > x), together       Time to fill = xy ÷ (y − x)

     Three pipes x, y, z (all inlets)                  Time = xyz ÷ (xy + yz + zx)

     Pipe fills in x hrs but takes t hrs more due      Leak empties full tank in x(x + t) ÷ t hours
     to leak

     A and B together in T hrs; A alone in x hrs       B alone = xT ÷ (x − T)

     A + B in x hrs, B + C in y hrs, A + C in z hrs    A + B + C = 2xyz ÷ (xy + yz + zx)

     Pipes opened alternately (A, B, A, B…)            Find work in one 2-hour cycle, then cycles
                                                       needed + remainder


  LCM method (fastest in exams)

     1. Take tank capacity = LCM of all the given times.
     2. Efficiency of each pipe = Capacity ÷ its time (negative for an outlet).
     3. Time = Capacity ÷ (sum of efficiencies of pipes that are open).

  Example: A fills in 12 hrs, B in 15 hrs, C empties in 20 hrs → capacity 60, efficiencies 5 + 4 −
  3 = 6 → tank full in 10 hours.


  9. Discount
  Discount is always calculated on the Marked Price (MP), while profit is calculated on Cost
  Price (CP).


     Concept                                             Formula

     Discount                                            MP − SP

     Discount %                                          (Discount ÷ MP) × 100

     SP after discount D%                                MP × (100 − D) ÷ 100

     MP from SP                                          SP × 100 ÷ (100 − D)

     Successive discounts a% and b%                      Single equivalent = a + b − ab/100

     Three successive discounts a%, b%, c%               SP = MP × (1 − a/100)(1 − b/100)(1 − c/100)

     Relation of MP, CP with profit P% and                MP ÷ CP = (100 + P) ÷ (100 − D)

  Ready values (successive discounts)


     Discounts               Equivalent single discount
